# Outreach email rules

These are not formalities - they determine whether a mailing is legal.

**Jurisdiction note:** these rules are verified against **Swedish marketing law
(marknadsföringslagen) and the GDPR**, i.e. Sweden/EU. If you operate in another
jurisdiction, verify your local rules yourself before sending - do not assume
these transfer.

## The rules

- **Company addresses (`info@`, `contact@`) and named persons in their
  professional role:** no prior consent required. Sweden applies **opt-out** for
  legal persons - you may send unsolicited B2B email, but must honor requests to
  stop.
- **Private individuals and private addresses:** require **active consent in
  advance** (opt-in). Without it, nothing is sent. No exceptions.
- **Every message must contain a working way to decline further contact** - a
  functioning address or mechanism to say no. This applies **even toward legal
  persons**, with no exceptions.
- **The content must be relevant to the recipient's professional role.** This is
  what keeps the mailing within *legitimate interest* under the GDPR - a named
  person's work email address is personal data, and role relevance is the basis
  for processing it.
- **The sender must be clearly identified** - legal name, registration number
  where applicable, and a contact route. **No misleading subject lines.**

## The opt-out list

`opt-out.md` in the CRM folder is the permanent record of everyone who asked not
to be contacted. Rows are **never removed** - permanence is the list's entire
function; an empty list means nobody asked, not that the file should be tidied.

**Check every recipient list against `opt-out.md` before a campaign goes out**,
and record the check date in the campaign file. A contact who opted out receiving
another mailing is the one CRM mistake that cannot be corrected afterwards.
